    Delivery¶

    Pacto delivery decides which version of each service runs in each environment, such as test and prod. It uses your contracts to tell a safe change from a risky one, asks a person only when it must and rolls back a version that fails its checks. Flux does the actual switch from one version to the next (Argo CD can do it instead, as an experimental option). Words in links are defined in the glossary.

    Delivery is opt-in. It is a separate install, and only with it does Pacto change what runs. To set it up for a service, pactify it: give it a contract and connect it to delivery.

    Alpha

    Delivery is new: its settings and commands may still change.

    What happens to a change¶

    A change is a request such as "run payments 2.4.0 in prod". A person, your build pipeline or an AI agent can ask for one. Every change takes the same path:

    flowchart LR
  A[Request] --> B[Checks before]
  B -->|safe| D[Apply]
  B -->|could break someone| C[A person decides]
  C -->|approved| D
  D --> E[Checks after]
  E -->|pass| F[Done]
  E -->|fail| G{Safe to roll back?}
  F -->|a later check fails| G
  G -->|yes, once| R[Rolled back]
  G -->|no| Z[Frozen until a person decides]

    Before it moves, Pacto checks that this version was published with pacto publish, Pacto's release command, and signed, which proves who published it. It compares the new contract with the one running and looks at every service in that environment that uses this one. If none of them would break, the change goes ahead on its own on an automatic level.

    After Flux applies it, Pacto checks that the running version does what its contract says. You can add health checks too, such as error rates from your monitoring. These checks keep running after release, so a problem found an hour later still rolls the version back once, whoever asked for it: the version that ran before is put back. The Stage freezes instead when there is nothing safe to go back to, the rollback itself fails, someone chose the running version by hand (a rollback, or a promote during auto-hold) or someone else already took the name of the rollback Pacto raises. It then waits for a person to roll back or unfreeze it, from the dashboard or the command line. A failure of a version Pacto shipped with no person also costs the Stage one level: see Step-down. None of a Stage's last 50 failed versions is tried again there.

    Stages and records¶

    A version reaches prod only after it passed in the environment before it, such as test. Each service in each environment is called a Stage, and moving a Stage to a new version is called promoting it. A Stage can promote on its own only once a person has raised it up the autonomy ladder.

    Every change that ends, refusals included, can leave a signed record in your registry. The signature shows the record was not changed later. Anyone can check who asked for what, who approved and what each check said. Records are off until you turn on the recorder, and they need a registry that serves OCI Referrers.

    Who is asked and why¶

    Each contract lists the services it depends on and the versions of each it accepts (its consumer range), for example "checkout accepts payments 2.x". That is how Pacto knows who a change can break.

    Say payments 3.0.0 removes a field. In prod, checkout and orders still accept only payments 2.x. The change waits and Pacto asks only the checkout and orders teams, whose services it would break.

    A breaking change that no contract depends on still waits for a person, who can accept the unknown, because a service without a contract may call it. A Stage whose every consumer has a contract can say so with gates.impact.declaredConsumersOnly: true.

    Pacto also says what to update first. If checkout 1.9.0 accepts both payments 2.x and 3.0.0 and already passed in test, Pacto's plan says to ship it first. Then only the orders team is still asked.

    Pacto judges by declared version ranges, per consumer. Traffic shows which service calls which; Pacto has no view of the fields or endpoints each call uses. Why you were asked lists every case in which a change waits for a person.

    Who may approve¶

    AI agents can plan, ask for and cancel their own changes, but only people can approve. The person who asked cannot approve, and neither can the person an agent says it acts for (--on-behalf-of, which the agent claims and Pacto never checks). An agent that leaves it out hides who is behind it, so the two-person rule holds only for changes a person asks for directly or an agent asks for in their name.

    A person approves with the sign-in they have, and the record names them. You can also ask for a recent sign-in to decide. That needs a cluster that trusts your company login, untested on EKS, GKE and AKS; where approving works says how to check and what to do without it.

    What Unknown means¶

    Every check answers Pass, Fail or Unknown. Unknown means Pacto could not see enough to say: too little monitoring data, a registry that did not answer, a breaking change that no contract depends on. Pacto sees only the consumers that have a contract, so a service with no contract may still call the one that changed.

    An Unknown check holds the change for a person, who can reject it or accept the unknown. The record marks that check as accepted and names who accepted it.

    Where you follow it¶

    • The dashboard, the web page pacto dashboard starts, gains a Delivery tab once it knows who you are (you sign in, or run it on your own machine): the changes waiting for your team, what each environment runs, past changes with who asked and who approved, and the publishes Pacto refused.
    • The command line: pacto delivery plan shows what would happen before you ask, and pacto delivery history what already did. kubectl get pacto-delivery -A lists every Stage, Operation and Approval; name a kind in full, such as stages.delivery.pacto.trianalab.io, where another tool also defines a Stage.
    • AI agents get the same tools through pacto mcp --delivery. MCP is the common way AI agents call tools. There is no approve tool.
