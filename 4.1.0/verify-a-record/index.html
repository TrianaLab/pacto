    Verify a record¶

    This page is for an auditor or security reviewer who must check what Pacto did, using standard tools only. It shows how to read a record, check its signature, check the whole history and check that a released version was signed. Without access to the cluster you still take three things from the team that runs Pacto: the public key records are checked against, the address where a Stage's records hang and the id of its newest record (a hash of that record, explained in Check the whole history). Ask for all three in writing and keep them: a later mismatch is evidence. Two optional checks below need one more key each: the eval runner's for exams and the publisher's for released versions. The full detail is in Signed records and history.

    Words in links are defined in the glossary: a record is signed by the recorder and stored in your registry as a referrer of a digest. Each one is about one change to one Stage. A Stage's records form its stream, numbered and hung on one fixed digest, its anchor. A follower is a Stage in another cluster that takes the versions this Stage verified by reading its stream.

    What a record says¶

    A record answers: what was asked, by whom, for which Stage, what each check said and why, who approved and how it ended. Refusals are recorded too. It holds references, never secrets. A record is an in-toto statement with the type delivery.pacto.trianalab.io/operation/v1.

    Every field sits under .predicate:

    Field What it says
    kind operation for a change, gap for a placeholder (see below), unrecordable for a signed stand-in for a change whose own record failed a check. A stand-in takes that change's number in the chain and hangs on the anchor only. A follower takes no more versions from the stream at a stand-in until a person acknowledges it (an acknowledge record on that Stage names its record id in intent.params.record)
    intent What was asked: the change's name, its Stage (intent.stage.namespace and intent.stage.name) and its reason
    actor.user, actor.class Who asked, and whether that is a human, an agent, break-glass (a member of the emergency group, whose change skips every check and approval and freezes the Stage) or Pacto itself (controller)
    actor.onBehalfOf The person an agent said it acted for. Only claimed, never checked, and marked so
    approvals[] Who answered (by), the answer (decision: approve, reject or accept-unknown, which means a person let the change go ahead although a check said Unknown) and when they last signed in (authTime)
    verdicts[] Each check (gate), when it ran (phase: pre before the move, verify after) and its result: Pass, Fail or Unknown
    verdicts[].citation.runner, .runnerBundle For an eval check: who ran the exam, and the answer that runner signed itself (see "Check who ran an exam")
    outcome.result How it ended, such as Succeeded, Failed, Rejected or Cancelled
    outcome.pointer.before, .after The version digest the Stage ran before and after
    guarded Read this first. true when the cluster's admission policies confirmed who asked and who approved. Without it, those names are only what the writer claimed
    chain.seq, chain.parent The record's number in its Stage's history and the id of the record before it (see "Check the whole history")

    Records exist only when your platform team turns on the recorder.

    Where records live¶

    Each record is stored in your registry as a referrer, attached to the version the change moved to, the version it moved from, the new contract and one fixed digest per Stage called the anchor. These four digests are the record's signed subjects (.subject[], named to, from, contract and anchor). The anchor is where the whole history of one Stage hangs. All anchors live in one repository, the streams repository. The anchor's full address is in the Stage:

    kubectl get stages.delivery.pacto.trianalab.io -n prod payments -o jsonpath='{.status.stream.anchor}'
# registry.example/pacto/streams@sha256:3a7f...

    If you have no cluster access, ask the platform team for this address. It never changes for a Stage.

    Get the public key¶

    You check signatures against the recorder's public key, a file such as recorder.pub. Get it from the platform team, not from the registry, so a forger cannot hand you their own. The platform team makes it with one of these (you need neither):

    openssl pkey -in key.pem -pubout -out recorder.pub          # from a key file
cosign public-key --key awskms:///alias/pacto > recorder.pub # from a cloud KMS key

    If the recorder signs with no key file (keyless), ask instead for its certificate identity, issuer and trusted root; the records reference shows the flags.

    Check one record without Pacto¶

    You need oras, cosign, jq and base64, and read access to the registry (oras login registry.example). List the records on the anchor, fetch one and check its signature. Records are the entries of type application/vnd.dev.sigstore.bundle.v0.3+json; --artifact-type lists only those:

    ANCHOR=registry.example/pacto/streams@sha256:3a7f...   # from the step above
oras discover --artifact-type application/vnd.dev.sigstore.bundle.v0.3+json "$ANCHOR"
REC=sha256:...                                         # the digest of one entry in the list (not the record id below)
REPO=${ANCHOR%@*}
oras blob fetch --output record.json \
  "$REPO@$(oras manifest fetch "$REPO@$REC" | jq -r '.layers[0].digest')"
cosign verify-blob-attestation --bundle record.json --key recorder.pub \
  --type delivery.pacto.trianalab.io/operation/v1 --insecure-ignore-tlog \
  --digest "${ANCHOR##*sha256:}" --digestAlg sha256
jq -r .dsseEnvelope.payload record.json | base64 -d | jq .predicate   # read it

    --digest is the anchor's digest: the check passes only if this record names this Stage's anchor among its signed subjects, so a record moved from another Stage fails. cosign prints Verified OK when the signature holds and exits non-zero when it does not. --insecure-ignore-tlog is expected: the recorder never writes to a public log, because records name your services. The signature alone proves the record is unchanged.

    Check the whole history¶

    Each Stage's records are numbered 1, 2, 3 and so on (.predicate.chain.seq). A record's id is sha256: followed by the SHA-256 of its signed statement, the decoded payload:

    jq -r .dsseEnvelope.payload record.json | base64 -d | shasum -a 256   # the id, without "sha256:"

    oras discover lists a different digest for the entry. That digest names the stored file, and the id names what was signed. Compare ids with ids.

    Fetch and check every record on the anchor as above, then check three things:

    1. The numbers run 1, 2, 3 with no hole and no number twice.
    2. Each record's .predicate.chain.parent is the id of the record numbered one lower. Record 1 has no parent.
    3. The newest record's id equals the Stage's .status.stream.head, which the platform team can read for you. If it does not, newer records are missing.

    A record with .predicate.kind gap is a signed placeholder for a number no change used. It fills its number, so the stream stays continuous. Ask the platform team about every gap. With cluster access, Pacto does the same check in one command and exits non-zero if anything is wrong:

    pacto delivery record verify -n prod --stage payments --trust recorder.pub
pacto delivery blame -n prod --stage payments --trust recorder.pub   # who asked and approved what runs now

    When Pacto cannot read every record, its answer says partial. Then a missing change may still have happened: no answer means "unknown", never "nobody".

    Check who ran an exam¶

    An eval runner runs each eval check, such as an AI agent's eval suite. The runner signs its own answer: which version it ran, the sha256 of the suite, how many cases passed and where the run can be read again. The record keeps that signed answer in the verdict's citation.runnerBundle, inside the recorder's signature. So you can check the runner's signature yourself, without trusting the recorder or the promoter. Get the runner's public key from the team that runs the evals, not from the record or the registry:

    jq -r .dsseEnvelope.payload record.json | base64 -d \
  | jq -r '.predicate.verdicts[] | select(.citation.runnerBundle) | .citation.runnerBundle' > runner.json
VERSION=$(jq -r .dsseEnvelope.payload record.json | base64 -d | jq -r '.subject[] | select(.name=="to") | .digest.sha256')
cosign verify-blob-attestation --bundle runner.json --key runner.pub \
  --type delivery.pacto.trianalab.io/eval/v1 --insecure-ignore-tlog \
  --digest "$VERSION" --digestAlg sha256
jq -r .dsseEnvelope.payload runner.json | base64 -d | jq .predicate   # what it signed

    The commands take the first eval check of the record; a record with more than one keeps one signed answer per check. Then check what the runner signed against the record:

    • operation is the record's .predicate.intent.uid and the subject its to digest
    • the counts and the suite match the verdict's citation
    • the verdict's result follows from the counts and the Stage check's minPassRate in the record's .predicate.policy (a model the runner named can only make it worse)

    An eval verdict that says Pass or Fail with no runnerBundle has no runner signature. Do not trust it. With cluster access, pacto delivery record verify --trust keys.yaml does all of this when that file lists the runner's key with role: runner beside the recorder's, and prints who ran each exam. It finds the eval verdicts from the record's Stage policy and citations. To decide that yourself, list on the runner key the providers whose verdicts it must sign, as the trust config in Verify records shows.

    What this cannot catch: a promoter that reuses an answer the runner signed earlier in the same operation, for the same version. Each answer carries the one-time value the promoter asked with, and only the promoter knows which one it used last.

    Check a version was signed¶

    Every version Pacto promotes was published and signed with pacto publish. A break-glass override can skip this: look for actor.class break-glass in the records and check its to digest the same way. pacto publish signs each digest it pushes: the contract, each image and the files Flux applies. Check any of them with cosign and the publisher's public key, which the team that publishes gives you:

    cosign verify --key cosign.pub registry.example/acme/payments@sha256:...

    For keyless signing, from CI with no key file, check the identity instead:

    cosign verify --certificate-identity https://github.com/acme/payments/.github/workflows/release.yml@refs/heads/main \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com \
  registry.example/acme/payments@sha256:...

    If the team publishes with transparencyLog: false, add --insecure-ignore-tlog to the key command; the publishing team can tell you which it uses. pacto publish refuses to publish a version it cannot sign.

    To tie a record to a release, take the digest of the subject named to (jq -r '.subject[] | select(.name=="to") | .digest.sha256' on the decoded statement; it prints the bare hex, so put sha256: in front of it for cosign verify). It is the deploy artifact, the package of Kubernetes files Flux applies. It lives in the repository the service's contract names for that artifact, for example registry.example/acme/deploy/payments. Ask the publishing team for that address, as for the anchor. Check it there with the cosign verify command above. Get the publisher's key out of band too, never from the registry. See Publish a version for the signing settings.

    Known limits¶

    • Someone who controls both the registry and the cluster can delete the newest records and report an older newest id. Compare with the id you kept from your last audit.
    • With no public log, nothing outside the record proves when it was signed. Someone holding the recorder's key could write a record with any date.
    • A change running while its Stage is deleted is never recorded.
    • Two different names on a record do not always mean two people. When actor.class is agent and actor.onBehalfOf is empty, the person behind the agent may be the one who approved. Even when it is set, it is only the agent's claim.
    • Registry clean-up rules can delete records. The platform team must keep the streams repository out of them.
    • A record made while the cluster's admission checks were off has no guarded: true.
    • A runner's signature proves which runner key signed the answer, not that the runner ran every case honestly, and not which model really answered. A runner that reports observedModel vouches for it with its own key.
    • A mira or http verdict is unsigned, and the record signs only what the promoter read. Mira counts telemetry the service reports about itself (its own server spans, keyed on the service.name and service.version it sends), so code under test, or any pod that can reach the OTLP endpoint, can hide its errors or flood clean spans to force a Pass, or a Fail and with it a rollback. See what a health verdict can prove.
