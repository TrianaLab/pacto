    Try it on Kubernetes¶

    This demo runs Pacto delivery on a real Kubernetes cluster on your machine and follows one service, payments, through its releases. Use it to see every part working before you try your own service. The parts are real: the registry, Flux, delivery, the recorder, a health check, the node agent (which sees calls between services), sign-in and the dashboard. The services themselves, their traffic and the people signing in are made up (see What is generated). Its Stages start on fixed levels: payments on autonomous in test and prod, the other services on autonomous in test and advise in prod, and fraud on observe. To see a Stage earn a proposal to move up a level, use the browser demo.

    Run it¶

    You need Docker with a builder that can export OCI images (docker buildx create --use --driver docker-container), kind, kubectl, Helm, Go, Node, oras, cosign, jq, openssl and GNU timeout (on macOS, brew install coreutils), and a clone of the Pacto repository. It builds the images from the clone. Host ports 3300, 8585, 5556, 8443 and 5602 must be free.

    make e2e-delivery-demo-up      # the cluster and everything on it
make e2e-delivery-demo-story   # play the story, checking every step

    up takes about 20 minutes, most of it building the images; story about 8. story stops with payments 3.0.0 held for prod and prints what to do next. When you are done:

    make e2e-delivery-demo-down

    What you see¶

    The story starts with one service that has no contract yet. The guided path is five moments, in the order the story plays them:

    • Pactify a service. pacto init --delivery reads fraud's Dockerfile and manifests and writes its contract, its Pacto resource and its Stages. --no-transparency-log keeps the demo's throwaway key out of the public transparency log, and --insecure-registry lets Flux read its plain HTTP registry. The demo uses the files as init wrote them. Both of fraud's Stages start on observe: Flux applies the tag, running the image pacto publish built, pinned by digest, and Pacto only notes that it would have shipped 1.0.0 to test with nobody involved.
    • A safe change ships on its own. payments 2.4.0 adds an endpoint and reaches prod with nobody involved, because checkout and orders accept any payments 2.x.
    • A bad release is caught and rolled back. The prod error ratio rises after 2.4.0, so the health check fails, cites its query and payments rolls back. 2.4.2 calls fraud, which its contract never names, so it fails in test and never reaches prod. Each failure of a version Pacto shipped on its own steps payments down one level on that Stage, to auto-nonbreaking.
    • A breaking change asks only the affected teams. Publishing a removed field as 2.4.3 is refused: it needs a major version. payments' team ships 3.0.0 in test, since that Stage now waits for a person on a breaking change. 3.0.0 is held for prod, and only checkout and orders are asked. checkout 1.9 can ship first. From 2.4.2, payments declares the values of each environment its Stages deliver it to, and each plan shows that they pass.
    • Every step is a signed record. History reads each step back from signed records, and pacto delivery record verify checks them.

    Each step is checked against examples/story, the scenario every demo shares, and the demo stops with an error if the cluster does something else.

    Explore more¶

    Open http://127.0.0.1:3300 and sign in through Dex. Every password is password.

    • carol@example.com is on team orders, the team asked about payments 3.0.0.
    • alice@example.com is on team payments and is never asked.
    • pay-agent@example.com is an agent: it can look and propose. Only people approve.

    make e2e-delivery-demo-browser plays the rest in a browser, once per cluster, and checks it against examples/story:

    • checkout's team ships checkout 1.9 first, and the held payments 3.0.0 then asks only orders.
    • carol approves.
    • 3.0.0 sends too little telemetry, so its check says Unknown until carol accepts that. She then rolls 3.0.0 back, and automation holds off.
    • The newest signed record of payments in prod is deleted from the registry, and the Stage freezes. A changed copy of a record would be ignored, since anyone who can push could attach one.
    • pay-agent cannot unfreeze the Stage. alice does, once the record is back.
    • ops pins a version by hand with break-glass rights. That shows as a witnessed record and freezes the Stage again.
    • pay-agent ships as a service of its own, with its eval suite as the check: 1.0.0 passes its evals, but Pacto cannot see its external model, so alice accepts the unknown. 1.1.0 swaps its model; a rollback would return to a version never verified in test, so alice accepts that unknown too. 1.1.0 passes 1 of its 2 evals and rolls back in test to 1.0.0, where alice accepts the unknown again. The runner signs each count with its own key, the rollback cites that signed count and pacto delivery record verify checks the runner's signature beside the recorder's.

    story also prints the commands for pacto delivery blame and undo and for the agent over remote MCP.

    Another cluster and Argo CD¶

    make e2e-delivery-demo-reach adds a second cluster, prod-us. Its payments Stage follows prod in the first cluster through prod's signed records, and Argo CD applies what it promotes. Each controller works on its own: prod-us reads prod's signed records from the registry. In the demo that registry runs in the first cluster and prod-us reaches it through a NodePort. It takes the newest version prod verified and refuses 2.4.0 because it failed after release there, so run story before reach. Then prod-eu, a third payments Stage in the first cluster, takes 2.4.0, which fails after release and rolls back to 2.3.0; 2.3.0 then fails its re-check too. A Stage is rolled back only once, so prod-eu freezes, and the freeze is a signed record naming 2.3.0. A Stage in prod-us that follows prod-eu's records never takes 2.3.0, and alice unfreezes prod-eu. See Argo CD and several clusters.

    What is generated¶

    The payments workload is a stand-in (podinfo). Its telemetry comes from a span generator in the cluster, and the call 2.4.2 makes to fraud comes from a sidecar in that version. pay-agent's eval runner is a stand-in that answers fixed counts and signs them with a key the demo makes. Sign-in uses the demo's own Dex and Keycloak. When the record is deleted and restored, alice and ops act through admin credentials that impersonate them, with a sign-in time the script makes up.

    To see the operational graph on its own, run make e2e-operational-graph-up.
