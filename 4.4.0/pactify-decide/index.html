    Decide, unfreeze and roll back¶

    Alpha

    Delivery is new: its settings and commands may still change.

    This page is for the person Pacto asks to decide on a change. It covers everything you need to do it. If you set up Pacto for your team, read Pactify a service first.

    Words in links are defined in the glossary. Commands below name a Stage by its service and its environment's namespace, so -n prod means "in prod". A change is a Kubernetes object of kind Operation.

    Why you were asked¶

    Pacto holds a change and asks a person in these cases:

    • It would break a consumer. For example, payments 3.0.0 removes a field and the checkout contract accepts only payments 2.x. Pacto asks the team that owns checkout, because theirs is the service it would break. The same holds when checkout requires a guarantee from payments that the new version does not meet, wherever checkout runs and also when Pacto cannot see where it runs.
    • Its contract does not back the Stage's environment. The contract lists environments, but none named like the Stage's spec.environment, or that environment's values fail their configuration schemas. The service's team approves it, alongside any consumer team the change also waits for.
    • It could not check something. Monitoring had too little data, or no contract depends on the part that changed, so a service without a contract might call it. That check says Unknown.
    • The Stage is set to ask for every promote. This is approval.always, which pactify turns on. A person approves every promote, whatever the level.
    • The Stage is on advise. A person approves every promote that a person did not ask for; see the autonomy ladder.
    • The Stage is on auto for non-breaking and the change is breaking, possibly breaking or unclassified. Every consumer's contract accepts it, but this level ships only non-breaking changes on its own, so a person approves it.
    • The Stage requires records and the recorder cannot write them now. A person approves the promote or rollback to go ahead before its signed record exists. The change itself runs as usual.
    • Someone asked to raise the Stage's level. A raise lets Pacto do more on its own and keeps the running version. A person of the team that owns the service, other than the one who asked, approves it.
    • The version changes who owns the service (the team, or the DRI when no team is named). The team that owns the service now approves it, so the author of a version never picks who approves it. A version that only edits the owner's contacts does not wait. When the running contract cannot be read, no team is asked, and only break-glass can answer if the promoter, the part of delivery that runs each change, requires an owner team.
    • The version number is too small for the change. A breaking change published as a minor (1.9.0 after 1.8.0) would pass every ^1 range, so a person approves it. So does a version whose contract moved to another registry domain, because consumers declared against the old domain would not be counted.
    • A person or agent asked to roll back to a version a consumer has moved past. That consumer's team approves it first; see Cancel and roll back.

    Pacto asks members of the owner team of each affected service, or the changed service's own team when none of them names one, and skips the person who asked. If an AI agent asked for you without naming you, leave that change to a teammate; the rule is in Who may approve.

    Look, then decide¶

    Find the held change and read what Pacto asks:

    kubectl get operations.delivery.pacto.trianalab.io -n prod   # PHASE shows Held
pacto delivery explain -n prod <change>                       # what is asked and why

    The question: line explain prints ends with what it asks for, for example Approve shipping it anyway? or Accept the unknown?. Give that answer, or reject.

    Then give one answer:

    Answer Use it when What happens
    approve The question says "Approve" and you accept it. Pacto asks that in every case in Why you were asked except a check that said Unknown Once every team asked has approved, the version goes ahead, or for a raise, the Stage moves up one level
    accept-unknown The question says "Accept the unknown": a check said Unknown and you accept the risk The version goes ahead and the record says "accepted". If the version already shipped and a later check said Unknown, only this or reject answers
    reject You do not want this version here If it has not shipped, the change ends and the Stage keeps the version it runs. If it already shipped, Pacto rolls it back to the version before. Someone can ask again later 
    pacto delivery approve -n prod <change> --decision approve   # or accept-unknown, or reject

    <change> is the name in the NAME column of the first command. All three answers go through approve; the --decision flag says which one.

    A held change that nobody answers ends on its own after 24 hours. If it had not shipped, it ends as Expired and the Stage keeps the version it runs. If it had shipped and the question was about a later Unknown check, the Stage freezes instead, so a person looks at it. The person who asked can withdraw it at any time with pacto delivery cancel -n prod <change>.

    In the dashboard (pacto dashboard), the Delivery tab's Inbox lists the changes Waiting for your decision. Changes another team may answer too sit behind a Shared with other teams count, and frozen Stages and changes that could not finish behind their own counts. Open a change: the page leads with the question and two cards, If you approve and If you reject, each saying what happens and each with its own button. Write your note, then press Approve shipping (or Accept the unknown) or Reject. The checks, the affected services and the timeline are folded below, one click away.

    What you need to be allowed¶

    Three things, all set up once by your cluster admin:

    1. A role. Your Kubernetes user or group needs the approver role, and your group must be the owner team written in the contract of the service you are asked about: for a breaking change, the consumer that would break; otherwise, the service whose version or level is moving. Ask your admin for a binding like this one, for the team payments:
    kubectl create clusterrolebinding payments-approvers \
  --clusterrole=pacto-operator-delivery-approver --group=payments
    1. The people group. If your admin named the groups people are in when turning delivery on (humanGroups), your login must be in one. Anyone outside them is treated like an AI agent and can never approve.
    2. A recent company login, if your admin asks for one. By default you decide with the sign-in you have. If your admin sets delivery.promoter.authFreshness (a recent sign-in to decide), the cluster must trust your company sign-in and you must have signed in within that time. Then pacto delivery approve prints an address, you sign in again in your browser and it sends your answer with that login, and the dashboard asks you to sign in again in the same way. Before the first time, set your company's sign-in address, which your admin gives you, in your shell: export PACTO_DELIVERY_OIDC_ISSUER_URL=https://login.example.com.

    Where a recent sign-in is required but the cluster cannot check it (where approving works), nobody can give any of the three answers, reject included. A held change then has three ways out: the person who asked cancels it and publishes a version that asks nothing, it ends after 24 hours, or someone in the break-glass group forces it (below).

    When a Stage is frozen¶

    A frozen Stage shows true under FROZEN in kubectl get stages.delivery.pacto.trianalab.io -n prod, and its reason says why: What happens to a change lists when a failed version freezes a Stage instead of rolling back. A forced version (below) also freezes it. Fix the cause first, then let automation move it again:

    pacto delivery propose -n prod --stage payments --type unfreeze --reason "fixed the config"

    An unfreeze takes effect at once and needs nobody else, even though the command is propose. It also clears HOLD, so run it on a Stage that is on hold even when FROZEN shows false. It needs the approver role but no fresh login, and only the team that owns the service (the owner team in the contract running on the Stage) or break-glass may unfreeze it: a person of another team is refused with NotYourTeam, even with a cluster-wide approver binding. In the dashboard, the Stages view has an Unfreeze button on each frozen Stage, shown only to the people the same rule lets unfreeze it.

    Roll back by hand¶

    Pacto rolls a failed version back on its own. To remove a version that passed its checks, find the change that put it there and undo it:

    pacto delivery blame -n prod --stage payments   # the "change:" line is the change to undo
pacto delivery undo -n prod <change>            # prints the exact rollback command; changes nothing

    Run the command undo prints. If the Stage has moved on since, undo prints a normal change to the earlier version instead, and every check runs again. After a manual rollback, automation stops moving that Stage (the HOLD column shows true) until a person unfreezes it or asks for a new version, so your choice stays in place. In the dashboard, the History view has an Undo button on each change.

    Force a version (break-glass)¶

    Someone in the break-glass group can force a version past every check. It shows in the history and freezes the Stage until a person unfreezes it. Use it only when nothing else works. <change> is the held change whose version you force:

    to=$(kubectl -n prod get operations.delivery.pacto.trianalab.io <change> -o jsonpath='{.spec.params.to}')
pacto delivery propose -n prod --stage payments --type override --to "$to" --reason "why"
