    Delivery reference: signed records and history¶

    Alpha

    Delivery is new: its settings and commands may still change.

    The recorder writes a signed record of every operation that ends, refusals included, into your registry next to the versions it touched. Off by default. Only the recorder can sign and push records. With it on, history, blame and undo read the records in place of the Operations left in the cluster.

    What a record is¶

    An in-toto statement of predicate type delivery.pacto.trianalab.io/operation/v1: what was asked, by whom and for whom, the Stage policy, every check result with its proof, every approval and the outcome. It holds references, never secrets. It is a sigstore bundle attached as an OCI referrer to the version moved to, the one moved from, the new contract and the Stage's stream anchor. guarded: true says the admission policies checked who asked and who approved. The promoter admits an operation only while its admission policies guard the cluster, so every record it writes carries it. A record from an earlier promoter that ran without them lacks it, and its names are only what their writers claimed.

    An eval verdict carries two signatures:

    • the eval runner's, on its own answer, kept in citation.runnerBundle (eval providers), with the runner named in citation.runner
    • the recorder's, on the whole record around it

    Streams, stand-ins and gaps¶

    Each Stage has one stream: records are numbered (seq) and each names the one before (parent). An unrecordable record stands in for an operation that failed a check before signing (a value that looks like a secret, an approval no longer bound); status.record.refused says why. It keeps none of what the operation did, so record verify reports it as a problem, and a Stage in another cluster that follows the stream offers nothing from then on: the stand-in could be the failure that marks a version failed. The follower's Ready message names the stand-in's record id. A person of the team that owns the service checks what the operation did upstream, then acknowledges that one stand-in on the follower:

    pacto delivery propose -n prod --stage payments --type acknowledge \
  --record sha256:... --reason "checked upstream: the rollback failed nothing"

    The acknowledge takes effect at once and is recorded like any other operation, with who asked, the record id and the reason. The follower then skips that stand-in and offers versions again; any other stand-in still stops it. Only a person of that team or break-glass may acknowledge, never an agent or the promoter, and a reason is required. When nothing runs on the follower yet, or what runs names no team or cannot be read, only break-glass may acknowledge, whatever requireOwnerTeam says. Break-glass can still move the follower with an override instead. A gap record marks a number no operation holds. A record can be late but is never skipped: an Operation keeps its delivery.pacto.trianalab.io/record finalizer until the registry lists its record.

    Install¶

    Your registry must serve the OCI Referrers API natively. zot does; GitHub's registry and released distribution do not, so they hold no records.

    delivery:
  recorder:
    enabled: true
    streamsRepository: registry.example/pacto/streams
    registryCredentials:
      secretName: recorder-push       # a kubernetes.io/dockerconfigjson Secret
    signing:
      ed25519:
        secretName: recorder-key      # holds key.pem, a PKCS#8 Ed25519 key
      allowOperatorReadableKey: true  # the operator can read this Secret too

    The push Secret needs write access to the streams, deploy and contract repositories. Create a test key with openssl genpkey -algorithm ed25519 -out key.pem. The operator reads every Secret, so the chart refuses such a key without allowOperatorReadableKey, and refuses a recorder that shares the promoter's ServiceAccount.

    In production, sign with a KMS key (delivery.recorder.signing.kms: awskms:///alias/pacto; ECDSA P-256, Ed25519 or RSA PKCS#1 v1.5 with SHA-256; AWS, GCP, Azure and Vault) or keyless (delivery.recorder.signing.keyless: certificate identity and issuer, a Fulcio, a required timestamp authority and a trustedRoot). Workload identity goes in delivery.recorder.serviceAccount.annotations. The recorder checks a probe record at startup, so a wrong key stops it before anything lands.

    Check it works¶

    Each Stage gets a RecordStoreReady condition:

    Reason Meaning
    ReferrersSupported Records are being written
    ReferrersUnsupported The registry has no Referrers API
    RegistryUnavailable The registry did not answer; it tries again
    SignerUnavailable The signer (KMS key, keyless token, Fulcio or timestamp authority) failed; it tries again
    StreamConflict Two trusted records with one number, a broken chain or a missing head (the one the Stage names). The recorder stops and the Stage freezes

    A record signed by no key trusted for its stream is not part of its history: the recorder and followers skip it, and the condition names the first one. Anyone who can push can attach a referrer, so an untrusted one never freezes a Stage.

    Pacto never resolves a StreamConflict for you. Find the bad record with pacto delivery record verify, delete its referrer manifest from the streams repository, wait for RecordStoreReady to turn True, then unfreeze. After an etcd restore the recorder continues from the newest record.

    Require records¶

    By default automation keeps moving a Stage while the recorder cannot write, and records catch up later. To stop that, set records.required:

    spec:
  records:
    required: true

    Then every promote or rollback an agent or the promoter raises waits for a person's approval while RecordStoreReady is not True, or while more than five of the Stage's operations wait for a record. People, break-glass and freezes are not held. With delivery.recorder.monitoring.enabled, the chart scrapes the recorder and ships a PrometheusRule. PactoDeliveryRecordsPending fires when a stream has waited 15 minutes for a record, and PactoDeliveryRecorderDown when no recorder answers a scrape.

    Trust, key rotation and revocation¶

    Readers and the recorder check every signature against a trust config. How to write it, rotate the recorder's key and revoke a leaked one is in Delivery reference: record keys.

    History, blame and undo¶

    pacto delivery history -n prod --stage payments --trust recorder.pub
pacto delivery history --all --trust recorder.pub
pacto delivery blame -n prod --stage payments
pacto delivery undo -n prod <operation>

    Every answer says when it was read (asOf), its source (records, or status when only the cluster's Operations could be read) and its completeness: complete only when every record verifies and the chain is whole. problems lists what the records show is wrong, and unreadable what could not be read. On a partial answer a missing change may still have happened. --all walks every Stage you can list, up to 500 in two minutes.

    blame names the change that moved the Stage to what it runs: who asked, who approved, what each check said. No change on a partial history means unknown, never nobody. undo proposes nothing; it returns the plan and the command that would undo an operation. pacto delivery plan also lists what the new version's records say happened on other Stages, so a version that failed in test shows when you plan it for prod.

    Verify records¶

    pacto delivery record verify -n prod --stage payments --trust recorder.pub

    It checks every signature and the whole chain, exits non-zero if anything is wrong and prints a command that checks one record without Pacto, with oras and cosign. record verify --all checks every stream.

    Eval runners:

    • A record with an eval runner's signed answer verifies only when the trust config also lists that runner's key with role: runner. A keyless runner takes identity, issuer and trustedRoot.
    • The output names who ran each exam.
    • An eval verdict that says Pass or Fail fails the check when it keeps no runner-signed answer, when the answer is for another operation or version, or when the signed counts and the Stage's minPassRate give another result.
    • providers on a runner key lists the gate providers whose verdicts a runner must sign, so you decide which verdicts are exams.
    • A runner key verifies only runner answers, and a recorder key only records.
    • To check a runner's signature with cosign, see Verify a record.
    keys:
  - name: recorder
    publicKey: |
      -----BEGIN PUBLIC KEY-----
      ...
  - name: evals-ci
    role: runner
    providers: [evals]
    publicKey: |
      -----BEGIN PUBLIC KEY-----
      ...

      seq 4    operation     sha256:9c1f... promote-pay-agent-1-1-0
           evals: the exam was run and signed by evals-ci

    Check one record without Pacto gives the oras and cosign commands for a recorder key file.

    For a keyless recorder it uses these flags instead of --key recorder.pub, with the entry's trustedRoot saved as trusted_root.json:

    --trusted-root trusted_root.json --certificate-identity <identity> \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer <issuer> --use-signed-timestamps \
  --insecure-ignore-sct

    --insecure-ignore-sct appears only when the trusted root lists no certificate transparency log, as in many private Sigstores: then a certificate carries no SCT and cosign needs the flag. The TSA timestamp is still checked.

    Retention and events¶

    A recorded Operation is deleted after delivery.recorder.operationTTL (a week), except the one a Stage runs. Nothing in Pacto deletes a record, but registry retention rules can, leaving history partial: keep the streams repository and the referrers out of them. With delivery.recorder.cdeventsSink set, the recorder posts a CDEvent for every record, best effort.

    Limits¶

    • The recorder notices a tampered record only when it next records on that Stage; record verify checks any time.
    • Deleting a Stage while a change runs leaves that change unrecorded. Wait for its changes to conclude first.
    • A follower in another cluster notices records removed from a stream's tail only against the newest record it already read (details).
    • No record goes to a transparency log such as Rekor. A keyless record's signed time comes from the timestamp authority the recorder must name, and the certificate is checked at that time.
