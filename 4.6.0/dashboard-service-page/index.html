    The service page¶

    Open a service in the dashboard to see everything Pacto knows about it on one page: who owns it, which contract it runs, what it depends on and where it runs. An empty area always says why it is empty.

    What the page answers¶

    The facts at the top name the service's domain, its owner, the contract it runs, its capabilities and the sources that saw it. The operational summary shows compliance, how certainly each target is matched to a revision, how recently it was observed and how the declared dependencies compare with observed traffic. The sections below list findings, observed traffic, resources, operational targets, environments, delivery, revisions, expected dependencies, dependents, signatures, policies, configuration, recent evidence and the limitations that make any of it uncertain.

    Owner and contract¶

    The Owner fact reads No declared owner when a readable contract revision exists and none of them names an owner. It reads Owner unknown when Pacto could not read any of the service's contracts and no operational target reports the contract it runs, so Pacto cannot tell whether an owner is declared. The fleet counts both cases as unowned, and both link to this service's unowned attention item. For an unknown owner that item asks you to make one of the service's contracts readable. A declared owner links to the revision that declares it, under Declared in, where the owner's contacts are listed. The Contract fact links the newest revision in use, or the newest known revision when nothing runs the service.

    A mark after an owner no revision Pacto read declares, including a resource's provider owner, says in its tooltip where it comes from.

    • Where the values come from, a fold closed by default, lists each field of the current revision with one mark whose tooltip says declared, accepted (and by whom), attested or inferred, with its source and when it was read. It shows for a service whose current revision carries a declaration or that replaced an inferred service through one. Inferred values are muted, and clearing Show inferred values hides them; the fold is then their one list on the page, in place of Inferred by Pacto. Below them, the inferred services the service replaced through a declaration and what the newest one's workload showed, or a count of those whose workload you cannot see.

    Dependencies¶

    Expected dependencies lists every dependency the contract declares. A dependency on a fleet service links to that service. An external dependency is delivered outside Pacto and shows the version the contract declares. An unresolved dependency matches nothing Pacto knows. Dependencies hidden by your Kubernetes permissions are counted and never named. The section count is the Declared dependencies count in the dependency drift row, which also counts the dependencies declared and observed, declared and never observed, observed and never declared, and unresolved. Outside accepted range counts dependencies whose provider version falls outside the range the contract accepts. Cannot be observed counts dependencies Pacto has no observation data for, so it cannot tell whether the service talks to them.

    Resources¶

    Each card is one resource the service consumes.

    Part Shows
    Heading The provider, linked, its offering or no offering, the kind of use, the accepted range and the provider's lifecycle. A use of a provider with no offering appears once an adapter reports on it
    Provider owner The provider's owner and whether the provider answers where it runs, apart from this resource's phase
    Requested (declared) On the configuration row only: the provider revision, the configuration entry and the values the contract asks for
    Provisioning (observed) The phase per environment with the adapter, signed or unsigned and the external reference. Select a phase to see the request as a drawing. A phase is never the service's health; see Resources a service consumes
    Usage Not reported with the reason, and a link to the service in Mira when Mira is configured
    Below the cards At least N resources from providers you may not see, counted from this service's own contract and reports, then the sentence: Resize or delete runs in the provider's tools. pacto_edit refuses a request the provider's schema rejects, and pacto validate and push fail on it

    Targets and delivery¶

    Each operational target shows its environment, or Environment unknown when no source reported one. The Delivery section lists the service's Stages: the version each environment runs and whether it is verified, failed, frozen or waiting. A Stage belongs to the service when its spec.service.name equals the service name exactly, so orders never shows the Stages of orders-api. A Stage that names a registry domain in spec.service.domain shows only on the page of the service in that domain. A Stage that names no domain shows on the page of every service with that name. When the service has a domain, such a row reads Stage names no domain: the match is by service name only. When no Stage matches, the section says so: delivery is opt-in per service. When the dashboard does not serve delivery, the section says why, and when Pacto cannot reach the cluster it shows the error with a retry. When Pacto could not list every namespace, the section reads No Stages of this service that you can see and names what it could not list.

    Stages, actions and why¶

    Each Stage row shows its state, the version it runs linked to its revision, the contract behind it and the source revision pacto publish stamped (an annotation, not signed). A Stage that cannot promote says why and how to fix it. The row shows the newest re-check, marked stale when it is about an earlier version, and the newest change. A change read from an Operation's status is marked from status, not signed.

    The buttons under a row are the actions you may take: promote, unfreeze, freeze and undo. An action you may not take stays disabled, with the reason beside it. Every action proposes with your identity. Why asks the cluster, as you, what holds the Stage back, with each fix as a command when you may run it.

    Environments¶

    The Environments section lists each environment the current revision declares or a target you can see reports, alphabetically. It never shows a promotion order: the Delivery section does. Each fact says whether it is declared, observed or inferred.

    Cell Shows
    Declared Whether the environment's values pass, its lifetime (Lifetime not declared when absent, never persistent), its expiry, or Expired (inferred, as of ...) once expires has passed, and its owner: declared, service owner, inferred or Owner unknown. An environment only targets report reads Not declared by the current revision
    Observed The targets you can see labelled with the environment, linked to that target list, with (incomplete) and the reason when you may not see every target, and the revisions they run
    Provisioning The phases of the resources in the Resources section there (including those whose provider declares no offering), Unknown until a provider reports or why provisioning is not tracked there. A provider you may not see makes the cell Unknown
    Stage On a dashboard that serves delivery, the Stages whose spec.environment equals the name exactly, with their state. Stages unknown: partial listing when Pacto could not list every Stage. A Stage environment that matches no row is listed as Stage only

    Below the rows:

    • Targets with no environment label, or whose sources disagree, form one Unknown row.
    • Differences between environments lists each value the declared environments disagree on, one row per environment, with not set for a value an environment leaves out.
    • Environment changes opens the What changed in this service? section filtered to environment changes; see a service's changes.
    • An expired environment is never deleted; see temporary environments.

    Delivery links¶

    The Delivery history and Open in Delivery links open the delivery history and the environments board narrowed to the same Stages. Both pages name the service they show and link back to every service. The dashboard API filters Stages the same way with the service and domain parameters of /api/delivery/environments and /api/delivery/history.

    Golden paths and created services¶

    The page of a golden path adds a Golden path section:

    • Create a service from it holds a copyable CLI command and MCP call that name the path's newest revision by digest when a registry served it, or its folder when it was read from local files, or says why no command is available;
    • Signature shows the path's signature check: who signed it and whether a publisher rule, pin or policy accepts that signer;
    • Plugins lists the generate plugins the path names;
    • Requirements lists the offerings and references the path asks for, each reference that does not resolve and how many are on services you may not see;
    • Services created from it lists the services whose declared origin matches the path by content digest, and counts those you may not see.

    A service created from a golden path shows a Created from fact labelled declared: its contract records the origin, and Pacto did not observe the creation. It links to the golden path and its revision, or shows the recorded ref with the reason it matches no golden path. The Creation status section lists the creation checklist, derived from what Pacto observes now.

    Readiness, policies and configuration¶

    The readiness bar tallies every known revision as passing, below its own threshold, assessment expired or not assessed, and each bucket opens those revisions. Policies and Configuration show what the current revision declares. For a schema inside the bundle, the page shows its title, description and properties, read from the bundle when the page is requested. A policy that references another contract links to that contract, which holds its content. A configuration that references another contract links to it and, once the reference resolves, shows the provider, its version, the selected configuration and that schema's content inline. An unresolved reference shows why. A schema Pacto cannot read shows why too.

    Bundle signatures and referrers¶

    Only a registry source checks signatures. When the dashboard reads a revision from a registry, it lists the OCI referrers attached to the revision's digest, reads who signed each Sigstore signature and checks that signer against a publisher rule, a person's pin or a signed delivery policy (which publishers Pacto accepts). publish.sign never decides. Revisions read from a local directory, the cache or a catalog read Not checked.

    Status When
    verified A signature verifies and its signer is accepted by the installation's rule, a pin, a verified policy or, for an external system, its release signer
    unverified A signature verifies, and no rule, pin or policy accepts its signer. The page names who signed in the reason and the rule's identity as the Expected signer
    unsigned No Sigstore bundle is attached to the digest, or the attached bundles hold no signature
    unknown Pacto could not decide: the registry could not list the referrers or returned only their first page, the revision has no digest, the trusted root could not be fetched, the checks ran out of time or the rule was inferred. The reason is shown
    • source says what decided: rule, pin, policy, release, inferred or none, shown as a mark on the revision page's Checked against row.
    • When the dashboard reads the installation's publishers.json and pins from the cluster, it judges as the promoter does. Without them it infers a rule from where the revision lives, such as ghcr.io/acme, and an accepted signer reads unknown with source inferred: that rule matches any organization's CI on its own artifacts, so it never makes a signature verified.

    A key or KMS signer reads unknown because the bundle carries no public key and the dashboard never calls a contract's KMS. Legacy cosign .sig tags are not read. On GHCR it reads pacto publish's pacto-sig- tags, never the sha256-<hex> index.

    Each result is cached for 5 minutes, and a failed check for 1 minute. The checks of one refresh share a 20 second budget. pacto fleet runs the same check on each registry reference given with --oci. The result is the provenance field of each revision in pacto_fleet_get and in /api/fleet/entities/revision?key=<key>.
