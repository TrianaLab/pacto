One versioned contract per service

{# Plain words here, the precise term one screen down. This line is the first thing anyone reads, and "OCI artifact" was landing before anything on the page had expanded either word. Both halves of the product, in order: the contract is declared AND it is checked against reality. A tagline that stops at "versioned and distributed" sells a YAML file. The checking is what the page's own closing sentence leads with, and it belongs here instead. The list is the answer to "a contract containing what?", and it leads on the two items nothing else versions: ownership and a dependency carrying a compatibility range. Naming them beats "how a service is meant to behave", which a reader can fill in with anything. The opening clause is the strategic frame the eyebrow's category noun implies but does not state: what an operational contract system is FOR is giving software an interface a machine can read. The list stays — the frame without it is a slogan. #}

A machine-readable operational interface for every service: ownership, interfaces, version-ranged dependencies, policies and readiness — declared in one file, published to any container registry, then compared against the last revision and against what is actually running.
